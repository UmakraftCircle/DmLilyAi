# Uma Musume Guide For Absolute Beginners (detailed fact reference)

**Source:** https://gametora.com/umamusume/beginners-guide
**Author:** Gertas, published on GameTora (page last updated 2025-06-26)

## 1. Game overview

- Uma Musume: Pretty Derby is a gacha game with roguelite elements. Its main loop is a turn-based raising sim centered on (mostly automated) horse racing, with the racers being anthropomorphic horsegirls ("uma musume").
- A newly obtained character cannot be used in PvP or Daily Races right away. She must first go through **Career mode**, which is the core of the game.
- When a Career ends, the character's final stats and skills are stored as a **Veteran** version. Veterans are what other modes use. A player may keep several Veteran versions of the same character and can re-run a Career if unhappy with the result.
- The game plays very differently from a typical mobile gacha, since progress comes from repeated training runs.

## 2. Stamina (run limit) and premium currency

- There is a stamina-style limit on Careers, but it is generous: one full bar allows three Career runs.
- One run typically takes about 20 to 40 minutes, or longer if all text is read, so a bar partly refills during three back-to-back runs.
- Extra runs can be bought with premium currency at 10 gems per run, versus 150 gems per gacha pull. There is no daily cap on recharging. Free recharge items are also given out occasionally.
- The game suits both heavy play and casual play, such as a few runs a week.

## 3. Gacha ("Scouts")

- Two separate pools: **character (Trainee) scouts** and **support card scouts**.
- The guide stresses not to spend everything on characters: support cards make characters stronger and matter a lot.
- An exchange ("spark") system exists: after **200 pulls on one banner**, the featured character or card can be taken for free. (Threshold as stated for the Japanese server.)

## 4. Career mode setup

Entry: the large button at the bottom right of the main screen.

**Choosing the character.** The guide suggests Sakura Bakushin O as the easiest character to learn on, because she is forgiving while a new player learns the controls.

**Choosing Legacies ("parents").**
- Legacies influence several aspects of a run (details are in the GameTora legacy guide: https://gametora.com/umamusume/legacies).
- Any Veteran from earlier Careers can be used as a Legacy. This is one of the few things that carries over between runs.
- A Veteran can also be borrowed from someone on the Follow List as a Guest Legacy, up to **3 times per day**. This is especially helpful early on.

**Choosing support cards.**
- The player picks **6 supports**: 5 owned plus 1 borrowed from the Follow List.
- Supports act as training partners that give bonuses when they join a training. Each card also has its own random events.
- A support card that depicts the same character being trained cannot be selected.

## 5. Turns and actions

- A Career is split into roughly **70 turns**. Each turn the player picks an action.
- **Rest** restores 30, 50 or 70 energy at random. 50 is the most common result.
- **Infirmary** appears only when a negative status effect is active and has a high chance to cure it. Negative statuses mostly come from random training events.
- Other actions: train, go out, race. Buying skills does not consume a turn.

## 6. Stats and training

Five main stats:

| Stat | Effect |
|------|--------|
| Speed | Sets top running speed. |
| Stamina | Works like race hit points. Running out causes a sharp speed drop. Faster and longer running drains it more. |
| Power | Governs acceleration and also helps speed on uphill sections. |
| Guts | Mainly reduces stamina consumption. Often neglected in favor of raising stamina, but guts-heavy builds exist. |
| Wit | Mainly affects how often skills activate. Training at the wit facility costs no energy and gives a little back. |

Training rules:
- There are five facilities, one per stat. Training at one gives a large gain in its main stat plus a smaller gain in one or two others. These pairings are fixed (for example, power training always gives power and a smaller amount of stamina).

### Support interaction during training
- Support characters appear on facilities. Each turn a card's facility is randomly picked, though cards have a higher chance to appear at the facility matching their own type.
- Training with a support adds bonus stats according to that card's effect values (viewable in the support list: https://gametora.com/umamusume/supports).
- Training together raises a card's **friendship gauge**. When it reaches the final bar (shown orange), it unlocks the **Friendship Bonus**: training at that card's matching facility gives boosted stats, shown with a rainbow glow.
- A red exclamation mark on a support means a **hint** is available. Hints usually unlock a skill for purchase and discount it, and sometimes give stats instead.
- A few story characters can also appear at facilities. They give small bonuses and have their own friendship gauges. Filling one before the Career ends can grant extra stats.

## 7. Skills

- Skills are abilities used during races. There are hundreds (full list: https://gametora.com/umamusume/skills).
- Most must be bought from the Skills menu with **skill points**, but a skill must first be unlocked for purchase. Sources include sparks from Legacies, support hints, random events and races.
- Every character has some skills unlocked automatically. More can be "awakened" by raising **Potential Levels** with resources gained in trainings. Skills unlocked that way stay available for purchase in all later Careers, another thing that carries over between runs.
- Skill points come from training events and, most commonly, from racing.

## 8. Mood and outings

- **Mood** is shown at the top right. Higher mood gives more stats from training and improves race performance, so keeping it maxed is worthwhile.
- Going out (the yellow button) raises mood. The outing is random: shrine, karaoke or a walk.
  - Walk or shrine: also restores some energy.
  - Karaoke: gives two mood levels instead of one.
  - Shrine: can also cure a negative status.
- A hidden minigame can occasionally trigger during an outing.
- **Pal-type support cards** feature non-horsegirl characters such as Tazuna (the secretary) and Aoi (the young trainer). In training they lower energy consumption instead of adding stats. Each also has its own outings with better rewards than the standard ones, listed in the support list.

## 9. Racing

- Races are based on real Japanese horse races and run automatically once started. They can be skipped to the results.
- A Career has several mandatory races and many optional ones. Entering an optional race costs energy but pays out well, above all in skill points. It is a fallback for turns when training options are poor.
- On the race screen the player can view the stats of their uma and of NPC rivals, and can set a running strategy.
- Four strategies, listed on screen left to right:

| Strategy | Behavior |
|----------|----------|
| End Closer | Stays at the back and pushes in the final phase. |
| Late Surger | Similar to End Closer but sits less far back. |
| Pace Chaser | Stays ahead of that, leading the main pack. |
| Front Runner | Goes for the lead early and tries to win with a large gap. |

- Advice: use a strategy the character is suited to unless you know what you are doing.

## 10. Career goals

- Each character has predetermined goals that must be met to continue. They can be checked from a button at the top of the screen.
- Failing a goal ends the run and it must be restarted.
- Typical goals: finish no worse than a set placement in a specific race, or reach a fan count by a deadline. Fans come from placing well in races.

## 11. Career events

- After many actions, a visual-novel style scene may play. Very many exist, with unique ones for every trainee and support card.
- They show daily school life, cover the trainee, supports or the main story, and can be random or tied to a date (for example a first shrine visit in January).
- Outcomes usually give stats, skill points or status effects, but can be negative.
- Some events offer dialogue choices with different outcomes. GameTora's Training Event Helper covers these: https://gametora.com/umamusume/training-event-helper

## 12. Other game modes

### Daily Races
- 3 attempts per day. Two types: one gives money, the other gives support-card upgrade materials. Each has three difficulty levels, with higher difficulty giving more.
- Recommended: run the highest difficulty you can win, all three times. Rewards are modest but quick, and the races count toward daily missions.

### Legend Races
- Found under the "Race Events" button in the other-modes menu. Only available a few times a month, and the bottom right button on the race screen lights up when one is active.
- Usually runs for 3 consecutive days with 3 attempts per day.
- One opponent is marked as the "legend" and stays the same all three days.
- Rewards are character-specific **star pieces** (for example Special Week star pieces during a Special Week event), which permanently raise a character's rarity (star rating). The amount depends on placement, with a bonus for the first win during the event.
- Worth doing, and ideally winning, whenever available.

### Team Trials (PvP)
- Matches are against players near your power level. Each match has **five races**: sprint, mile, medium, long and dirt. Dirt is the same distance as mile but on a dirt surface. The others are turf.
- Because of this, a roster with varied Veterans is needed. A single long-distance Front Runner is not enough.
- Five teams are set up, initially with one character each, growing to three characters per team as you progress. They also act as your defense when others challenge you, with no penalty for losing on defense.
- One match costs 1 RP. RP regenerates at 1 per 2 hours.
- Races can be watched or skipped.
- Each race awards points. Winning at least 3 of 5 gives a victory bonus. Scoring detail: https://gametora.com/umamusume/team-trials-pvp-scoring
- Ranking counts only your **best score of the current week**, so even one strong match can rank well, while many matches are possible for top ranks.
- New players start in Class 1. A good weekly score moves you up the following week. There are 6 classes, with better end-of-week rewards at higher classes, plus first-time bonuses (the first time reaching Class 5 gives 1000 carats).
