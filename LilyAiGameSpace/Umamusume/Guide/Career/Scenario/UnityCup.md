Evaluating a Turn [​](#evaluating-a-turn)
-----------------------------------------

When choosing how you spend a Turn, there are several factors that need to be considered:

*   Career progress.
*   The Stats, Mood, Energy, and Skill Points of our Uma.
*   The Friendship value of our Support Cards.
*   The Scenario mechanics (In Unity, this would be Unity training and Spirit Bursts.)
*   Any worthwhile Secret Events for our Uma.

The most important element is our Career progress, since our priorities will shift as we get further into the Career. For example, in the beginning, we want to focus on raising the Friendship value of our Support Cards to 80 to unlock Friendship Training. Unity Trainings are also important for Spirit Bursts, which will level our Training Facilities.

Therefore, this guide will cover the Unity Cup scenario, split into three sections: Junior, Classic, and Senior Year.

Junior Year [​](#junior-year)
-----------------------------

### Pre-Debut [​](#pre-debut)

As previously explained, we highly prioritize Friendship Value and the Scenario mechanic during Junior Year. In Unity Cup, the Scenario mechanics are Unity Training and Spirit Bursts. Besides these, we also have to consider Mood.

Good Mood increases the stat gain of our trainings by 10%, and Great Mood 20%. This boost is further increased by the Mood Effect of our Support Cards, leading to a significant increase to stat gains overall. Consequently, our Mood should be Great as early as possible. There are two common approaches to achieve this:

Some will click Recreation on the first turn in order to roll the Karaoke Recreation event, which raises the Mood from Normal to Great and gives 0 Energy. However, if they roll either of the other Recreation events, they will receive only a +1 to Mood and between 10-30 Energy. This is not ideal, because the additional Energy will be wasted. Combined with the fact that Karaoke only has a 33% chance of appearing, this is a risky strategy.

Others prefer to train on the first turn and wait until Turn 2 or Turn 3 before they click Recreation. This is more consistent because even if we don’t roll Karaoke, the 10-30 Energy will not be wasted.

#### TL;DR: [​](#tl-dr)

1.  Click Recreation on Turn 1
    1.  33% chance to immediately get Great Mood
    2.  67% chance to get Good Mood, but waste 10-30 Energy
2.  Click Recreation on Turn 2 / Turn 3
    1.  33% chance to get Great Mood slightly later
    2.  No risk of wasting Energy

TIP

Due to the importance of Unity Training, using Recreation early can save Energy on the turns before they are unlocked.

Both methods have merit, so we can take a formulaic approach to see when to click Recreation. We can assign points to different factors and determine when to click Recreation based on the value of our Turns.

### Turn Scoring (Early Game) [​](#turn-scoring-early-game)


|Turn Evaluation (per Training)  |Points|
|--------------------------------|------|
|Friendship/Bonds                |1     |
|Unity Trainings                 |1     |
|Non-Support Card Unity Trainings|0.5   |
|Hint                            |1     |
|Wit Training                    |1     |
|Spirit Bursts                   |2     |


INFO

Because Wit Training is energy efficient while also being one of the most important stats, it has a base value of 1 point. If Speed and Wit Training both had one Support Card, we would click Wit.

Once a Support Card is Orange Friendship Level (80/100) it unlocks Friendship Training, also known as Rainbow Training.

From that point on, increasing its Friendship is no longer worth 1 point. Focus on Bursting and increasing the Friendship of other support cards.

INFO

As of February 5th, 2026, Pal Cards have been updated to no longer require green bond to start Recreation.

This means that Riko is much less valuable of a click in the earlygame, whereas prior, she had a point value of 2.

Generally, we would only gamble with Recreation on the first turn if none of our training facilities have a value higher than 1. Here is an example:

![First turn of a run with a good choice.](https://uma.guide/img/guides/article/Training.webp)

First turn of a run with a good choice.

As it’s only the first turn, the Unity Training mechanic is not available. Using the Turn Scoring method, Stamina, Power, and Wit have a score of 1, and Guts has a score of 3. Therefore, we would click Guts this turn, **not** Recreation. If Guts only had a score of 1, it could be worthwhile to click Recreation to gamble for Karaoke.

As we’ve opted to train Guts, our Energy bar is no longer full, and we will no longer waste energy if we click Recreation and don’t get Karaoke. Now, almost every outcome from clicking Recreation is worthwhile. With this in mind, the score threshold now increases from 1 to 3. On the following Turn, the only time we wouldn’t be clicking Recreation is if one of our training facilities has a score higher than 3.

![Turn 2 of the run.](https://uma.guide/img/guides/article/Training2.webp)

Turn 2 of the run.

In this example, our Speed and Stamina have a score of 1, and Power and Guts have a score of 0. Wit has a score of 3. Since all of our Trainings have a value of 3 or lower, we can click Recreation.

![Ahh look at her smile, she’s so pretty. Protect the smile.](https://uma.guide/img/guides/article/Recreation.webp)

Ahh look at her smile, she’s so pretty. Protect the smile.

Our Mood is now Good, and we didn’t waste any of the energy gained from Recreation! From here, we can repeat the logic for Turn 3 or 4 to get our Mood to Great: If all of the Trainings score 1 or below, we can click Recreation. Otherwise, we will organically raise our Mood through Support Card events, but this is dependent on our Support Cards. For example, SSR Kitasan Black Speed and SSR Nice Nature Wit both give a +1 Mood on their first chain Events. Both Riko Kashimoto Pal Cards will give a +1 Mood the first time you train with her, but she does not appear until Turn 5.

Now that our Mood is sorted, we can focus on training our Uma until the Junior Make Debut race. We’re going to continue using the established scoring method to determine what Training Facilities we should be clicking.

![A slightly more difficult turn.](https://uma.guide/img/guides/article/Training3.webp)

A slightly more difficult turn.

A situation like this can be very confusing for anyone new to Unity Cup. However, we can determine the correct training with our scoring method. On Power, we have two Support cards that would be gaining Friendship value along with one Unity Training, for a total of +3 points.

Guts, on the other hand, has one Support Card that would be gaining Friendship, so +1. Our Unity Trainings, however, are **not** on Support Cards. We only value these at half a point, so our total score for this Guts click is 2. In this situation, we would click Power over Guts.

From here on, Turns are relatively simple. We can’t run any optional races since it’s Pre-Debut, and we most likely haven’t unlocked Recreation with Riko this early on: Our only options are either to Train or Rest. If we’re still in a Good Mood, our first training with Riko Pal will raise our mood to Great. We can prioritize Riko in this situation to raise our Mood as fast as possible.

#### Energy Management [​](#energy-management)

Once our Energy bar has depleted a fair bit, Energy Management becomes important. We want to minimize Resting because turns spent Resting are turns spent not gaining any Stats or Skill Points.

![Energy at about 50/100.](https://uma.guide/img/guides/article/EnergyBar.webp)

Energy at about 50/100.

Turns where our Energy bar looks like this are critical points where thinking about Energy Management can make or break a Career. Let’s look at an example.

![Low-Energy Training Example.](https://uma.guide/img/guides/article/Training4.webp)

Low-Energy Training Example.

Ignore the Scoring method and look at this from an Energy-focused point of view. If we click Speed or Power, we will almost certainly have to Rest on the next turn. If we click Wit, however, we will have enough Energy to Train non-Wit for one or two more Turns. Carefully considering the value of Trainings that force us to Rest next turn is critical for the **entire career.**

### Post-Debut [​](#post-debut)

After our Pre-Debut turns and the Junior Make Debut, we’ve now unlocked the ability to run optional Races and the Agenda. To understand why this is important, we need to talk about Secret Events.

Most Umas in the game have at least one Secret Event that occurs when certain conditions are met. These conditions almost always revolve around winning Races. Let’s head over to Gametora and look up Daiwa Scarlet. She has a Secret Event called “Number One and the Coolest.”

![Daiwa Scarlet’s secret event, “Number One and the Coolest”. Grants stats, SP, and Hints.](https://uma.guide/img/guides/article/SecretEvent.webp)

Daiwa Scarlet’s secret event, “Number One and the Coolest”. Grants stats, SP, and Hints.

The rewards from this event are considerable: The Stats and Skill Points alone make this worthwhile. These rewards also scale with Race Bonus, making it even better. However, four of these races are not part of the standard Races Daiwa Scarlet will run during her career. To make sure we don’t miss these races, we’re going to use the Agenda to schedule these optional Races.

![The Race Agenda, which lets us schedule optional races & track when races are coming up.](https://uma.guide/img/guides/article/Agenda.webp)

The Race Agenda, which lets us schedule optional races & track when races are coming up.

Now that we’ve scheduled all of the optional Races we need, we don’t have to worry about accidentally missing one of them. It is worth mentioning that Daiwa Scarlet is originally scheduled to race in the Japanese Oaks, and we must choose the Japanese Derby on her Career Event to complete her Secret Event.

INFO

Failing to trigger the Secret Event for most Umas is not critical; it’s not worth giving up on the run for.

INFO

Some Umas, like TM Opera O, have Secret Events that can trigger multiple times. It is usually worthwhile to trigger the event as many times as possible.

Another factor to consider post-Debut race is that our Unity Trainings will have accumulated enough for a **Spirit Burst.**

#### Spirit Bursts [​](#spirit-bursts)

Spirit Bursts have a chance to appear once the Unity Training gauge is full on any member of the Unity team. When popped, they give additional stats and a Skill Hint matching any of the Uma’s A-rank Aptitudes, along with consuming additional energy. Spirit Bursts also significantly increase the stats of our Unity team members.

Early on in Unity Cup, Spirit Bursts are the biggest contributor towards leveling our Facilities. Popping Spirit Bursts immediately early on in the run is important for two reasons.

1.  After every Unity Race, we receive new members based on our current Unity team Stats. If we do not burst immediately, our new members will have lower average Stats and snowball the run negatively.
2.  Secondly, Spirit Bursts only have a 40% chance to appear each turn. If we do not pop Bursts immediately, there is a chance that they will not appear again until the next Unity Race. Therefore, it’s recommended to pop Spirit Bursts sooner, even if they fall on a suboptimal Training.

INFO

This is only true until around post-Summer Classic Year.

After that point in time, we can save Bursts to improve already strong Trainings.

A rule of thumb is to have our Unity Rank at C before Classic Year Summer, B before Senior Year, A by Jan Senior Year, and S by Summer Senior Year.

The remainder of Junior Year is fairly simple, using the Scoring method while considering Energy Management. Any turns worth 1 point or lower can be spent racing or going on a Riko Outing. Racing non-Graded Races, however, is ill-advised as it gives less rewards. We will talk more about Optional Races during the Classic Year.

Our scoring method does not change much between Junior and Classic Year. Most of our Support Cards should have high enough Friendship, and Riko’s Outings should be unlocked. The only other major change for Classic Year is Optional Races.

### Optional Races [​](#optional-races)

Optional Races are one of the most important uses for a Turn in the Career. They give a small Stat increase and, more importantly, Skill Points. Skill Points are how we buy Skills, and Skills are what separates a casual Uma from a competitive Uma. Ideally, we should be running anywhere between **15 to 22 Races per Career.** An Uma will run anywhere from 9 to 13 Races to meet her Career goals: We must make up for the rest with Optional Races.

In our Daiwa Scarlet example, running all of her default Races plus the optional Races needed for the Secret Event gets us to 12 Races. The URA Finale consists of 3 more races, bringing our total to 15. This is already a pretty decent number of Races and should net us anywhere between 1,600 to 2,000 Skill Points depending on our Race Bonus.

For Umas that do not have long Secret Events or Career Goals, we have to find a good time to race. A turn where there’s bad Friendship Training, bad Spirit Bursts, and high enough Energy is often referred to as a “dead turn”. On these dead turns, we can run optional Races without much opportunity cost.

Considering everything thus far, Classic Year is relatively straightforward. We want to look for high Friendship Trainings and Spirit Bursts while keeping Energy Management in mind. Until Classic Year Summer, we want to be popping Bursts as often as possible. We’re still trying to increase our Training Facility Levels.

### Summer Training [​](#summer-training)

Summer Training also affects our evaluation of a Turn. During the four Turns of Summer Training, all of our Training Facilities are max Level. To optimize our Summer Turns’ stat gains, we need to enter Summer with **as much Energy as possible**. In Late June, or Early June if we run the Takarazuka Kinen, our Energy needs to be at least 80% full and our Mood Great. Alternatively, if our Mood is Good and our Energy is low, we can also Rest on the first turn of Summer to increase our Mood and Energy at once.

With proper Energy management prior to Summer, we should be able to train non-Wit three times and Wit once. If we find ourselves with a dead turn during Summer, it’s worthwhile to click Rest & Recreation even if it would overflow slightly. If we have multiple dead turns during Summer, we can use the standard Scoring method to click the best possible training facility available. This is still worthwhile because we are training on a level five facility.

TIP

Rest & Recreation during Summer will cure all negative conditions on our Uma. This does not apply to Uma-specific conditions that are acquired through Career like Super Creek or Meisho Doto.

After Summer, the rest of Classic Year is straightforward. The only important consideration for the rest of Classic Year is around December. The first turn of Senior Year will start with the New Years Event, which can reward 30 Energy. Immediately after, there is the Raffle Event, which provides another 20-30 Energy. In order to fully optimize our Energy, we can Train or Race down to low Energy, then grab 30 Energy from the New Years Event and _another_ 20-30 Energy from the Raffle. If we are nearly full Energy by the New Years Event, we can instead choose 35 Skill Points. Regardless, it’s important to keep these Events in mind to stay Energy-efficient.

**Senior Year** [​](#senior-year)
---------------------------------

### Turn Scoring (Late Game) [​](#turn-scoring-late-game)


|Turn Evaluation (per Training)            |Points|
|------------------------------------------|------|
|Friendship/Rainbow Training               |2     |
|Pre-Burst Unity Trainings                 |0.5   |
|Pre-Burst Non-Support Card Unity Trainings|0.5   |
|Hint                                      |1     |
|Wit Training                              |1     |
|Spirit Bursts                             |2     |


Our Scoring methodology for Senior Year doesn’t heavily deviate from Classic Year. The only noteworthy change in our decision making is that in Senior Year, it is worthwhile to ignore a Burst until it falls on a good Training. Friendship Trainings will usually give more stats than a Spirit Burst while taking less Energy, and our facilities should already be leveled up to at least four. From this point on, the most important consideration is our Stat goals.

Stat goals will vary from Trainer to Trainer. Generally, however, we want around 600 Speed, and 480 to 550 in Stamina, Power, and Wit at the beginning of Senior Year. This is assuming that we are training the Uma for Medium or Long tracks. For Mile and Sprint, we can lower our Stamina expectations and increase our Power and Wit expectations.

Additionally, the URA Finale and End of Career Events will give us around 54 to 63 to all Stats, and an extra 20 to 27 in Stamina/Guts from Riko Pal. For example, if we’re training for a Champion’s Meeting with the Stamina requirement of 850, we don’t need to Train Stamina anymore if we have 750 by Senior Year Late May,.

The same logic can apply for other Stats. If we have 850 Speed in Senior Year Early March and our Wit is only 550, we have to remember that Wit Training also increases Speed. Training mostly Wit and some Power while only clicking really good Speed Trainings should get us up to 1130 Speed by URA Finale. From there, we can cap out our Speed through the URA Finale and End of Career bonuses.
