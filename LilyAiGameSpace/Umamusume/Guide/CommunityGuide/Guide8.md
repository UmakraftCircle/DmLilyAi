Scenario summary
----------------

Mecha scenario training-oriented and intended for mid/long builds. It has the following stat caps:

1750/1700/1500/1300/1300

The theme of the scenario is to train together with the robot Umamusume ST-2 (nicknamed Sati) who was created by the Umamusume researcher Sugar Lights. Upgrading Sati during gameplay will provide higher effects during training, and there are multiple options to tune her to suit your current preferences. Objective races and other training objectives for each character will play out normally.

Overall, the basic gameplay of the scenario is quite simple, but there are some intricacies when it comes to tuning Sati and using her ability to boost your training through a mechanic called overdrive.

### Research levels

![](https://docs.google.com/docs-images-rt/ALKuztb6R9Ej99fKQn3b1OdtscvT4hRd4RinhcnBbUDA8vN0AHFnxjJsgOlZgx5NFMCXdpVWR7gpvETuDTccEizSc0TMQTln5CAbvbT1Upzlo_2l3bu0z7SCCvs0E5NES_H5gmk_o_E3YZskXtdUU_HPyYACNQEfe1CnyNVSwnE=s600)

At the left of your training screen you will see a research level menu. There are five different research categories, roughly corresponding to each of the five basic stats. Raising your research levels is one of the basic objectives of this scenario, and they will be raised simply by clicking on trainings. Racing will also raise all research levels slightly.

The basic premise of raising research levels is that they contribute to the scenario-specific training bonus in the training category they represent. The higher the level, the higher gains you will see in that training.

Research levels have caps that discourage clicking one category too much, and these caps will be raised periodically during training. Your research level total also matters for some calculations, and at the sidebar in the menu you’ll see a counter that fills up as your total increases. Filling up the bar before each Upgrade event (explained later) is your basic scenario objective.

### Overdrive![](https://docs.google.com/docs-images-rt/ALKuztbEVThjY7kjKIYM7fvFAnrI7RG61QTiEDP4aLoLb6hC1xJsy6hV21zoShoI2OULtt3L7MX0dRW3SVhI8dnjinpO1OryjFrHzlfOJYcia6a9IBr6fiBm6121DLPEHhq7F5H1nLebD0uiQxLRwaC6EkR2YQ9y0brOlme74WM=s600)

On top of a training button you may also find a mecha gear icon randomly. Clicking on these kinds of trainings will additionally raise your overdrive gauge, found just below the research level menu. Friendship (rainbow) trainings will always have a gear on them. Resting, dating, racing, and the infirmary will also raise this gauge.

![](https://docs.google.com/docs-images-rt/ALKuztZ8Y-nrrwgv6Nc-kCothtLPcZKuBgC0-aWnrQV38DE76T_-zRweXendEDO1-t3fHyyHI4E1IsjpIjxMIOwV4dH-citb1OeFKfEvXwudX0721YiAK65uj6dTpTLXDp-ovFj5KTpL2pqoa1ohDyNHt7cfUMaB7ct9hvv2q3w=s600)

The overdrive gauge will be filled by one third for each gear training you click on. Filling it up fills out one of the overdrives in the middle of the gauge. Overdrives are powerful one-turn effects just like cooking in GFF or goddess’ wisdoms in GM. You can store up to two overdrives at once, and having two available prevents you from stacking more overdrive gauge.

Activating an overdrive will provide a number of different bonuses for the turn activated, and the effect will depend on what kind of upgrades you have made for your robot companion Sati. The only bonus that is always present is a +25% training boost during overdrives. Let’s look at upgrading next before we explore all the possible bonuses.

### Upgrade events

Upgrade events or UGEs happen during training every six months starting from the end of Junior year. The idea is similar to most other recent scenarios: You want to achieve a certain research level total beforehand to achieve S rating in each of these events and gain increased effects. This should be fairly straightforward if you play normally and don’t overcap on research levels too much.

![](https://docs.google.com/docs-images-rt/ALKuztavPxoJ7CqhPV9iSB4FkoYoybPr-vHUUP2XCWOCqElxaiv4oibD75GWzfkmlq2UkDge2tQNEXwk6S1_IFCkYeeXQRgbY-1bSsilz2LJPUFscbCA_oWWZChFo7HWoan2BBt5mO887mqJU4Zwz3VXp8S309W2P94QftyywOU=s600)

After each UGE, you’ll be able to access a tuning menu where you can allocate mecha energy (EN) to different cores inside Sati to enhance your overdrive effects and gain some passive bonuses. The cores represent the head, chest, and legs, and each core is further divided into three chips which can be upgraded individually.

On turn 3 you’ll gain some initial mecha energy and will access the tuning menu to grab some initial boosts. Since the tuning menu can only be accessed at this time and after each UGE, you should plan ahead to what bonuses will be most beneficial for your upcoming training.

Your initial mecha energy is 5 (higher with some scenario links), and S rating in each UGE gives 6 more. Your research level caps also increase by 100 per UGE and your training levels increase on the first and third UGE.

The key point about mecha energy allocation is that you can reset and re-allocate it after each UGE. This means that you can, for example, allocate into a core that raises the bond of your supports early and switch to other bonuses later. Each UGE will add additional energy and some chips will only be available later, so you can access stronger bonuses as your training progresses.

The table below lists what bonuses your overdrives gain from how much energy is allocated into each core in total. All of the effects below are overdrive only.



* Points total: 3
  * Head Core: Gear in all trainings
  * Chest Core: +1% training bonus per member in training
  * Leg Core: +3 bond gauge in training
* Points total: 6
  * Head Core: +3% int per 300 total research levels+20% boost to research level gain
  * Chest Core: +3% stam and guts per 300 total research levels
  * Leg Core: +3% speed and power per 300 total research levels
* Points total: 9(replaces 6pt bonus)
  * Head Core: +3% int per 200 total research levels+25% boost to research level gain
  * Chest Core: +3% stam and guts per 200 total research levels
  * Leg Core: +3% speed and power per 200 total research levels
* Points total: 12
  * Head Core: 50% stamina reduction in training
  * Chest Core: +3% training bonus per member in training (replaces 3pt bonus)
  * Leg Core: +15 energy+1 motivation
* Points total: 15
  * Head Core: Gain a hint from all supports in the training category
  * Chest Core: Two additional copies of support cards appear in training (random category)
  * Leg Core: +3% skill points per 150 total research levels


These are in addition to the base +25% training boost during overdrives and the passive stat-specific training boosts from research levels in the scenario. Boosts stack in a weird multiplicative way. The numbers you see in actual training are higher than what the UI numbers for the bonuses would indicate.

  * Bonus: Stam chip
  * Effect size per lvl: Increased research level gain (stamina)

  * Bonus: Increased research level gain (guts)
  * Effect size per lvl: 10/18/26/33/40 (%)

The specialty rate chip in the Head Core is only usable after the second UGE, so Classic summer. The friendship and skill point chips in the Chest and Leg cores are only active after the fourth UGE, so Senior summer.

The size of each boost depends on the amount of points allocated to each chip. The maximum energy that can be allocated into a single chip is 5. This means that the 15pt overdrive bonuses are only available from maxing all three chips in the corresponding core.

### URA Finals

You do not need to save your overdrives for the final three turns in URA finals, as a Super Overdrive will occur there instead on each turn. This works like a normal overdrive but activates for free.

If you fail the scenario objectives this will not activate, so be sure to achieve S rating in each UGE.

Scenario gameplay strategy
--------------------------

### Basic gameplay and goals

Raising bonds initially is as useful as always, and you can expedite this process by allocating 3 energy into the Leg Core (either speed or power chip) on turn 3. This will raise your bond gain from overdrives by +3.

Overdrives are strong enough that you should prioritize clicking on gear icons to some extent. After your supports are bonded up this becomes just a typical training loop of chasing rainbows as usual.

In the early turns when you’re bonding up supports, you need to be clicking on both big bond stacks and gear icons. It requires a bit of luck to get a good training each turn, but you can affect this by saving your overdrives for turns where a support stack is on a training without a mecha gear, since if you’ve allocated 3 energy to the Head Core you’ll gain a gear on every training during overdrives.

The general gameplay is fairly simple with not many decisions to be made. You want to click on a big training/rainbow each turn, but pay a little bit of attention that you raise research levels in a balanced way overall. Don’t overfocus on this though, since overcapping on one category a bit isn’t that detrimental overall. But you should definitely think about things like skipping small speed rainbows for a double rainbow later if you’re running two speed cards. Racing is also a pretty decent way to rack up skill points, and the scenario doesn’t punish you too much for doing it as you’re still gaining research levels and overdrive gauge when racing, so feel free to use your bad turns to race instead.

![](https://docs.google.com/docs-images-rt/ALKuztaT96u_jH_XBpLoOE5FOVya3Zjk3yfE-LB0m8-fU3HIhgrHn3c_G0x4VWJaX0hOAWiFAZ9lJrWciZYzm2TE1vrbuOqQfxJ-C8ToFWAFJit8a2DvP2Oi1lwXqyxVmH5UzlJu5RSPJ3K7jJmFwhNX--T5rBAKvPlBaLTXwp8=s600)

Overdrive usage will vary based on your needs, but it’s not too hard to manage these since you can stack up two of them at once. If you use an overdrive on the turn which fills the gauge towards your second one, or the turn after when the gauge is already filled, you’ll still be left with another overdrive for the next turn in case you need one. If you do it one turn beforehand, you’ll have to survive one turn without an overdrive available which is usually fine. Pay attention to objective races and resting turns which will fill the gauge so you can plan ahead and not overcap the gauge, as it will be wasted otherwise.

One simple thing to keep in mind is that rainbow training isn’t as encouraged as in some previous scenarios. You’ll typically be chasing rainbows since that’s where the gears and big trainings are, but a small rainbow over a big support stack with a gear on it might not be worth taking. Overdrive doesn’t discriminate against non-rainbows much either, the only notable bonus is the Friendship chip later on which gives a +10% boost to rainbows during overdrive.

Make sure to also think about your tuning bonuses, which will affect which training you want to overdrive on. If you’re all-in on int training boosts, your single int rainbow might be stronger than a stack on speed. But honestly, most of the time you can just look at the numbers above the training and decide based on which one looks better.

If you fail to achieve S rating in the UGE’s it’s probably because you had bad luck and lacked good trainings in certain categories. In this case just restart the run.

### Optimizing tuning

We list here the key points to consider for each of the six tuning events during training:

First tuning, turn 3

Recommended setup: 3/0/3

Allocate 3 points into the Leg Core (speed mainly, 1 point in power is fine too) to gain access to the bond effect. The rest is probably most reasonable to spend unlocking the 3-point bonus from the Head Core which gives you easier access to gears. You can go for 3 points in the hint rate chip for slightly faster bonding, or put 1 point in int for level gains. If you don’t have Air Shakur in your deck and lack 6 mecha energy to start with, you’re gonna have a sad time here.

1st UGE, Classic start

Recommended setup: Mixed

The exact tuning setup doesn’t matter all that much here, but you should have 3 points in the Head Core for guaranteed gears in overdrives. The rest should be allocated towards bonuses for trainings you want to be clicking on. If you’re feeling confident in clicking speed rainbows (and aren’t overcapping on speed research levels already), a setup like 6/0/6 might be decent for example. For stamina/guts/int, go for 6/6/0 instead. Adjust to your deck also. If you’re not running a guts card, you might not need to invest in the Chest Core for example.

2nd UGE, Classic summer

Recommended setups: 15/3/0 or a mixed setup

This is when the specialty rate chip becomes active. You should put 5 points in it unless you want to highroll and keep the head core at 3pt for some reason. You could max out the Head Core for a while here to take advantage of the hint gaining effect and energy discount on trainings. Purely in terms of stats it might seem better to invest in the other two cores, as their overdrive bonuses are stronger (as long as you’re actually clicking on speed/stam/pow/guts and not int), but the energy discount from the Head Core 12pt bonus does translate to a good amount of stats also. The additional skill hints will also be very useful for your final build, and if you overcap on hints for some supports you’ll just get stats from their hints later in the run which isn’t too bad either.

Note that for classic and senior summer, you should try to manage your overdrives so you ideally have two stored up for the summer turn. If you gain a gear on every turn in the training so far, this lines up perfectly. You should aim for three overdrives during the summer if reasonable.

3rd UGE, Senior start        

Recommended setups: 15/9/0 or a mixed setup

If you’re doing decently on speed and power, you could forego the Leg Core upgrades altogether here in order to focus on the other three stats and get strong overdrives throughout the spring anyways. If you really lack speed you probably have to try to fix the situation with a different setup.

4th UGE, Senior summer

Recommended setups: 0/15/15 or 6/9/15

The friendship and SP boost chips are available now, so you can go ham on those. The first recommended setup maxes out the chips in the Chest Core for the overdrive effect of additional supports gathering in your trainings. This is quite random though and you will miss out on the specialty rate boost from the Head Core and the guaranteed gears, so it’s a high risk high reward strategy. The 15pt bonus on the Leg Core is very important for additional skill points which are in high demand in PvP, and the 12pt bonus of +15 energy gained per overdrive will allow you to focus on training more.

If you’re feeling like you need more consistency and more int gains, go for 6/9/15 or 9/6/15 instead for a decent balance. Ideally your int should be at a decent point here though since you should have taken advantage of int rainbows while having your Head Core upgraded in the past 12 months.

As usual, save some overdrives for summer. This is the point where they get really strong.

If you want to maximize your skill point gains, you should be aware of the cap of 100 SP gained from the additional scenario boosts (the number on top of the usual training SP gain). This is easy to overcap during overdrives on strong trainings, so alternatively you can save overdrives for more average trainings (which will still overcap, but by less) instead of stronger ones to achieve a net gain in SP. Of course, this generally lowers stats gained by a bit, but it’s probably worth it regardless.

5th UGE, URA finals

Recommended setup: 6/15/15

Max out your training bonuses since you’ll want to take full advantage of the Super Overdrive during finals.

Deck choices and inheritance
----------------------------

### Deckbuilding

There is no scenario specialty friend/group card in Mecha scenario (and it is confirmed none will be added later). If your build allows for it, you should always try to include strong scenario link cards since their effects are strong here. Air Shakur (stamina) is almost a must in all mid/long decks, and chasers will highly benefit from running Taishin int.

Stamina was buffed in a balance patch during the scenario release, so for longer distances you’ll want to gather as much stamina as you reasonably can not just to survive but also because it increases your maximum speed. As usual, adjust your deck to your character's bonuses and gold skill needs as well.

A basic deck lineup will consist of a speed/stamina/int card as a baseline. You can then add a second speed card unless your uma has a very high speed bonus in which case you might reconsider. The last two cards are more flexible. Tsurugi Ryoka is a very solid option because she’s the strongest friend/group card option at base strength. Running two stamina cards is a solid option for longer distances, but for shorter mid races you might only use one if you have some gold skills you’d like to pick up from other cards. Three speed builds work too and are a good option if you have strong speed cards but lack good power/guts cards.
