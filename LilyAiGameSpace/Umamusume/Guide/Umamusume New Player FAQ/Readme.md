## 1. Rerolling

- Rerolling is fairly quick in this game, so it can be worth doing.
- Unless the goal is only to collect characters, reroll for **support cards**, never for characters. Supports affect account strength far more than any single character does.
- A free character selector ticket is available, so a specific favorite character does not need a reroll.
- Reroll on the **Standard support banner**.
- Popular reroll targets: **Fine Motion** and **Super Creek**. Aim for at least one of them, and more copies are better.
- Related step-by-step reroll info is in the sibling folder "Umamusume Global Quickstart Guide".

## 2. Support card terminology

- **0LB**: a card with no duplicates.
- **MLB / 4LB**: maximum limit break, a card upgraded with four duplicates.
- Duplicates matter a great deal. A max-limit-break SR card can beat most SSR cards that are at 0LB.

## 3. Support card notes (launch-era)

| Card | Role and notes |
|------|----------------|
| Kitasan Black | The most significant meta card of the first year. Rerolling for her is advised if you care about meta. If you skip her, you will be borrowing her in nearly every deck for a long time. Her banner was live at the time of writing. |
| Fine Motion | One of the two most popular reroll targets. Usable right away at 0LB, stays relevant for a long time, and improves noticeably with each duplicate. |
| Super Creek | The other most popular reroll target. A staple for medium and long distance umas early, thanks to a guaranteed stamina skill. Weak in stats at 0LB, so one extra copy for the Stamina Bonus at 1LB helps. |
| Biko Pegasus | Not a main reroll target but a nice extra alongside Super Creek or Fine Motion. |
| Sakura Bakushin O, Tokai Teio | The other two SSR Speed cards, helpful early. Together with Biko Pegasus, all three get noticeably stronger at 1LB. |
| Tazuna Hayakawa | Very strong, but needs at least 2LB (more is better) and falls out of the meta fairly early. Recommended only for players with spare money. Others should borrow her when needed. |

Some of the stronger SR supports were not yet available at launch, which made these SSR cards more relevant then.

## 4. Characters

- Any character you like is fine. Some are slightly more meta, but favorites will not ruin your account.
- Expect a free SSR character pick ticket relatively early, plus a random SSR ticket. Use the **random ticket first** (since it is random) and the pick ticket afterward.
- Rerolling for characters is not recommended. However, roughly **50 pulls on the character banner** after rerolling can be worthwhile, since that should yield all 1-star and 2-star characters and broaden your training pool.
- Launch-era options if you are unsure what to pick with the ticket:

| Character | Notes |
|-----------|-------|
| Oguri Cap | Arguably the strongest at release. An all-rounder usable everywhere except sprint races. |
| Maruzensky | Another all-rounder that can handle all content to some degree. |
| Taiki Shuttle | A Dirt race specialist. Dirt specialists are hard to find early. |

## 5. What to prioritize during a Career

Two core mechanics:

1. Raising a support's **friendship gauge to orange (80 points)** makes later training with that support glow rainbow, giving extra stats.
2. Training **four times at the same facility** levels the facility up, so later training there gives more stats.

Gauge gain per training with supports:
- +7 friendship per support present.
- +5 instead, if that support has a hint (exclamation mark).
- The Charming status adds another +2.

Suggested decision flow:
1. **Early in a Career:** focus on raising supports' friendship gauges over raw stats. Keep facility levels in mind. When options are about equal, choose the facility you want to level. Wit training is good because it costs no energy.
2. **Low energy:** rest.
3. **Most supports orange:** switch to stats through rainbow training. If a rainbow training exists and energy allows, do it.
4. **No rainbow available:** usually do an optional race. Races are good, and a G1 is better. Otherwise train Wit.
5. **Mood yellow or worse:** consider Recreation.
6. **A negative status is seriously hurting the run:** use the Infirmary, but there is no pressure to heal every status right away.

## 6. How much Stamina to build

Stamina needs depend on race distance, running strategy and track conditions.

### During a Career
The trainee has a hidden stat bonus in Career, so these are fairly safe thresholds for the **late** part of a Career. Earlier, much less is enough because rival builds also lack stamina.

| Distance | Stamina |
|----------|---------|
| Sprint | 150 |
| Mile | 350 |
| Medium | 500 |
| Long | 600+ |

### For PvP modes (outside Career)
Much more is needed, and some long distances outright require recovery skills.

| Distance | Stamina |
|----------|---------|
| Sprint | 550 |
| Mile | 750 |
| Medium | 1100 |
| Long (2500m) | 1350 |
| Long (3200m) | 1500 |

### Adjustments
- **Recovery skills:** each gold-rarity (upgraded) stamina recovery skill you trigger lowers the requirement. About 200 in Career and for non-Long PvP distances, about 240 for Long PvP distances. Activation is never 100% guaranteed.
- **Strategy:** Front Runners and Pace Chasers need about 5% more than Late Surgers and End Closers.
- **Bad track conditions:** add about 2%.
- **Guts:** the PvP table assumes about 500 Guts. More Guts allows slightly less Stamina.
- Very long distances cannot be sustained without recovery skills.
