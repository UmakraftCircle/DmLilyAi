## Overview

K.S.Miracle is a character in the Umamusume franchise and one of the four protagonists of the stage play Umamusume Pretty Derby: Sprinters' Story, alongside Daitaku Helios, Daiichi Ruby and Yamanin Zephyr. She is a gentle, calm Senior Division student in Ritto Dormitory who wants to repay the people who helped her survive, and she has promised to bring about a miracle for them. In the game she is a sprinter-leaning trainee with her best aptitudes in short-distance turf races.

## Background

- **Personality:** Kind-hearted, calm and refreshing. She has a gentle manner, but she is willing to put her body on the line when she races.
- **Motivation:** She has a strong wish to pay back those who saved her, which her profile ties to having survived two near-death experiences. She says the support of the people around her is why she gives everything she has each day.
- **Habits:** She returns borrowed things cleaner than she received them, and she always says thank you before leaving the waiting room ahead of a race. She plays piano well, a skill she picked up to cheer up lonely children she met at a hospital, and her phone background is a portrait from those children.
- **Relationships:** Daiichi Ruby is her roommate, friend and rival. Daitaku Helios and Yamanin Zephyr are friends and rivals, Bamboo Memory is a senior she helps with committee work, and Sakura Bakushin O and Calstone Light O are underclassmen who admire her.
- **Origins:** Her profile treats her as one of the few Umamusume born in the United States who can speak English, a nod to the real horse's birthplace. Her liking for bananas is another nod to the real horse.
- **Debut:** She was first announced during the 1.5th Anniversary PakaLive TV stream on 21 August 2022.

### Career notes (per Namu Wiki, JP data)

- **Cancelled Classic Sprinters Stakes:** Her Classic-year Sprinters Stakes objective is cancelled by a story event. She is hospitalized, only resting is possible for that stretch, and Hayakawa Tazuna appears on the training screen in her place. The next-objective display briefly goes blank. Namu Wiki notes many players are surprised by this on a first run.
- **Real-horse basis of the objectives:** Because the real horse's career was very short, most objectives are "what if" races. She only raced three of them in real life: the Centaur Stakes, the Swan Stakes and the Mile Championship.
- **Difficulty:** Nearly every objective requires 1st place. The Classic year is mostly G2 and G3 races, but the Senior year is all G1 races, so it is the harder half.
- **Unique title:** Her title is Miracle Leader (奇跡の立役者). It requires winning the Senior Sprinters Stakes, Speed and Guts both at 1200 or higher, and more than 320,000 fans. Namu Wiki says the fan count is the hard part, since short-distance and mile runners gain fans more slowly.
- **Unique race call:** Winning the Senior Sprinters Stakes triggers a special announcer call about a miracle and being the fastest of the era. Her Climax scenario call is also unique and is about repaying a debt of gratitude.
- **Random event chain:** A three-part chain called "Under the Elevated Bridge" appears. In the first two parts, choosing the lower option ends the chain. The third part has no choice and gives a hint for Sprint Gear.

**Unique skill (per Namu Wiki, name machine translated as "Blue light of happiness"):** activates when she is in the front part of the field (roughly the top 20-50%) at a random point in the first half of the final stretch, and raises her speed. The boost matches the last sprint well. It is the standard unique-skill strength (0.35) in Short and Mile races and one tier lower (0.25) in Medium and Long races, so it is weaker if inherited onto a Medium or Long runner.

**Build notes (per Namu Wiki):** She is a Short/Mile Pace Chaser. Her own skills and the unique skill's placement condition all suit Pace Chaser, so there is little reason to change her style even though Late Surger is B.

Her owned skills are Short or Pace Chaser skills, and the unique skill's extra effect is Short only. Mid-race competition is common in Short and Mile races, so the trigger fires often.
