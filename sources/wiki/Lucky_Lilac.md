To that end, she puts in conscious effort to act with grace at all times, trying to suppress her inner voice that protests it.

## Appearance

Lucky Lilac has long straight dusky-rose hair that is tied up in a neat side ponytail, decorated with floral accessories. She has straight bangs with white highlights which resembles the markings of her real-life counterpart that cover her forehead, as well as two side bangs. She also sports two ear covers that have star shaped earrings on them. Lucky Lilac has light-magenta colored eyes. 

In her race outfit, she sports a white sleeveless halterneck shirt with detached sleeves, and a black silvered corset. She also wears a brown high-low skirt, with white knee-high socks. On her feet are two black boots with purple heels and a purple-colored flower in the middle of their shafts.

## Real-life page (excerpt)

Source: https://umamusu.wiki/IRL:Lucky_Lilac

' is a Japanese racehorse that was active from 2017 to 2020. She is best known for her victory in the 2017 Hanshin Juvenile Fillies, alongside her short-lived rivalry with the Triple Tiara winner Almond Eye, who went on to win nine G1 races in total. She also won the Osaka Hai in 2020, and won the Queen Elizabeth II Cup consecutively from 2019-2020.

### Name Origin

Sunday Racing describes the name as "A five-petal lilac flower (symbol of luck)." In many cultures around the world, five-petal lilacs, rare compared to commonly found four-petal lilacs, are eaten for good luck and happiness.

### Background

Lucky Lilac was born on April 3rd, 2015 at Northern Farm, and she came from her sire Orfevre's first crop of foals. Orfevre had won six G1 races, including the triple crown. Lucky Lilac's dam, Lilacs and Lace, was sired by Flower Alley, who won the Travers Stakes in the United States, and has two other G1-winning brothers-Tosen Ra and Spielberg. Lilacs and Lace herself won a G1 as well; the American G1 Ashland Stakes in 2011. 

Lucky Lilac was later purchased by Sunday Racing. As Lucky Lilac grew older, she was sent to train under Mikio Matsunaga at Ritto Training Center.

### Racing career

=== Two-year-old season (2017) ===
Lucky Lilac made her debut on August 20th in 1,600-meter turf race at Niigata Racecourse. After winning her debut by one length and a half, she went on to win the Artemis Stakes (G3) on October 28th, her first graded victory.

Her final race of the year was the Hanshin Juvenile Fillies on December 10th, her first G1 race. Breaking out at the homestretch, she won the race by three-quarters of a length. Earning her first G1 victory, Lucky Lilac received the JRA Award for Best Two-Year-Old Filly for her efforts.

=== Three-year-old season (2018) ===
Aiming for the Triple Tiara, Lucky Lilac started off the year with a victory in the Tulip Sho (G2) on March 3rd. As she held a good position during the race, she broke out at the final straight and won by two lengths. With this victory, she gained priority entry to the Oka Sho, the Triple Tiara's first leg. 

On April 8th, Lucky Lilac competed in the Oka Sho. As the overwhelming favorite, she ran within a good position throughout the race. Using her usual strategy, she broke out of the stretch and briefly held the lead. However, her win streak came to an end when Almond Eye, the second favorite who won the Shinzan Kinen (G3) this year, chased behind her and overtook her by three-quarters of a length. 

Suffering her first defeat, Lucky Lilac then competed in the Japanese Oaks on May 20th. At the 400-meter mark, both her and Almond Eye began to run side by side, and by the 200-meter mark, Almond Eye surged past her and won by two lengths. After the race, she took a break for a couple of months.

While Lucky Lilac was initially scheduled to compete in the Rose Stakes (G2) in the fall, it was discovered that she had swelling in the ball node of her right leg. Not only that, Shu Ishibashi, her usual jockey, was injured, so Yuichi Kitamura replaced him during the Shuka Sho. 

On October 14th, Lucky Lilac entered the Shuka Sho as her final race of the year. While she ran within the pack as usual, she did not accelerate at the final straight, and not only did she lose to Almond Eye once again, she also placed 9th, the lowest in her career.
